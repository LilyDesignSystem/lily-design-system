GOV.UK Design System

Maintained by a team at the Government Digital Service. Its home page lists Styles, Components and Patterns, shows GOV.UK Frontend 6.6.0 (released 15 October 2026), and puts its content under the Open Government Licence v3.0 except where stated. It names no accessibility standard on that page; it links an accessibility statement instead.

Lily takes a visual reference from it (the GOV.UK GDS theme) and carries no endorsement. Of Lily's 428 survey rows, 76 match a GOV.UK component. If you need actual GOV.UK compliance, use GOV.UK Frontend: it is the real thing, maintained by the organisation that owns the service standard. Moving from it to Lily: the GOV.UK slug-mapping guide.

design-system.service.gov.uk

U.S. Web Design System

Describes itself as "an active open source community of government engineers, content specialists, and designers"; the site is operated under the General Services Administration. Its home page lists 48 components and offers version 3.14.0 for download. It states no accessibility standard, conformance claim or licence on that page.

Lily has a USWDS theme as a visual reference. 123 of Lily's 428 survey rows match a USWDS component. If you need an accessibility conformance statement or VPAT, look at USWDS first: Lily targets WCAG 2.2 AAA, runs automated checks and has never been audited.

designsystem.digital.gov

Adobe Spectrum

Adobe describes it as "the system behind Adobe experiences". Its site has dedicated areas for React and Web Components (both marked beta), with iOS, Android and other platforms listed as coming soon. The home page states no component count, accessibility position or licence.

Spectrum is a design language with token systems, Figma kits and guidance; Lily's Adobe Spectrum theme is a reference, not that language. 152 of Lily's 428 survey rows match a Spectrum component. Choose Spectrum when you want a design language your team can adopt wholesale.

spectrum.adobe.com

Ant Design

Built by the Ant User-Experience Design Team for Ant Financial's enterprise products, and used, its site says, by Alibaba, Tencent and others. It states four design values (Natural, Certain, Meaningful, Growing). React is the official implementation; the Angular (NG-ZORRO), Vue and Blazor ports are community projects.

That last point is the sharpest contrast with Lily, which keeps one catalog, one set of slugs and one class contract across its frameworks in a single repository, rather than through separate community ports. 187 of Lily's 428 survey rows match an Ant Design component. Choose Ant Design for a complete enterprise design language on React.

ant.design

shadcn/ui

Built by shadcn at Vercel and open source. Its site says it is "not a component library" but how you build one: you get the component code itself through a CLI and a flat-file schema, rather than installing a package, so any part can be edited. It also says its open code is designed for LLMs to read and improve. The site does not name the primitives or styling it builds on, its frameworks or its licence.

Lily supports copying components into your repository too, but shadcn's tooling for it is better. 152 of Lily's 428 survey rows match a shadcn/ui component. Choose shadcn/ui when you want to ship a good-looking React app this week; Lily deliberately hands you no design.

ui.shadcn.com

DaisyUI

A free, open-source Tailwind CSS plugin that adds semantic class names such as btn, card and toggle. Created by Pouya Saadeghi with community and sponsor contributions; pure CSS with no JavaScript dependency. Its site lists 68 components, 600+ utility classes and 35 built-in themes, and offers an MCP server, an agent skill and an llms.txt file.

DaisyUI matches the most of Lily's 428 survey rows of any system: 194. The designs differ: DaisyUI ships the visuals, Lily ships none in its headless layer and keeps themes separate. Lily's review of DaisyUI records what it adopted, including token-only themes and AI-facing files.

daisyui.com

DHCW Design System

From Digital Health and Care Wales, in private beta (its "What's new" announces the release of 23 October 2026). It lists 28 components, nine design principles (one of them "Design services in Welsh and English", giving Welsh speakers and English speakers equal standing), a Roboto type scale, a colour page and page templates for public-facing and staff services. Each component is shown as HTML and as a Nunjucks macro. Its accessibility statement claims WCAG 2.2 AA after an internal test in October 2026; its content is under the Open Government Licence v3.0. The site names no package, version or source repository for its code.

Lily's two NHS Wales themes follow its link, hover, visited and active colours. The review also produced Lily's overflow mode for navigation menus, its Focus Not Obscured (WCAG 2.4.11) rule and a 30 percent Welsh text-expansion test, and recorded where Lily differs: its date picker, bilingual layout guidance (the site gives none) and its tag colours. It is not yet in Lily's component survey. The full review.
